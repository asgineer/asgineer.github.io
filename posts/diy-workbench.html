Building a Mobile Workshop Workbench

📅 November 4, 2023 ⏱ 9 min read ✏️ asgineer

My workshop had a problem: every horizontal surface was also a storage area for whatever landed on it most recently. I needed a proper workbench — one that could be moved, levelled, stored under exactly, and actually keep things organised. After two failed attempts with off-the-shelf solutions, I designed and built my own.

Design Goals

  • Work surface: 120 × 60 cm at ergonomic working height (90 cm for me)
  • Integrated face vise for woodworking
  • Four locking swivel casters — fully mobile when unlocked
  • Lower shelf + lockable cabinet with three drawers
  • Built-in power strip (4× socket, 2× USB-C)
  • Total material cost: under €350

Design Process

I modelled the entire bench in SolidWorks before buying a single piece of timber. I'm an engineer; guessing dimensions isn't in my DNA. The model let me run an FEA check on the leg joints and confirm that 44 × 44 mm pine with M10 bolts would handle 200 kg static load without meaningful deflection.

The corner joints use a mix of pocket screws (Kreg Jig), glue, and M10 carriage bolts for the primary frame — overkill structurally, but I wanted it to last a decade.

Bill of Materials

  • 44 × 44 mm pine battens × 12 m — frame
  • 19 mm birch plywood, 120 × 60 cm × 2 sheets — top + shelf
  • 18 mm MDF, assorted — cabinet sides and drawers
  • Locking swivel casters, 75 mm, × 4 (rated 80 kg each)
  • Record 52½ quick-release vise
  • Drawer slides, full extension, × 3 pairs
  • Cabinet lock + door hinges
  • Screws, bolts, glue, sandpaper, danish oil finish

Total spend: ~€310

Build Sequence

1. Frame

Cut all pine to length with a mitre saw. Assemble the leg pairs first with pocket screws and glue, then join with the horizontal rails. Check square obsessively at this stage — errors compound badly.

2. Worktop

The top is two layers of 18 mm birch plywood laminated together with PVA and clamped overnight. This gives a 36 mm thick slab that resists racking and feels solid under a mallet. Rounded the edges with a ⅜" roundover router bit, then three coats of danish oil.

3. Cabinet & Drawers

The lower cabinet runs full-width below the shelf. Three 150 mm drawers on full-extension slides for small tools and hardware. The door has a simple cam lock — not high security, but keeps dust out of the stored items.

4. Vise

The Record 52½ is bolted through the underside of the top with four M10 bolts into a 44 × 68 mm backing block. The vise jaw is flush with the worktop surface when fully open. This was the most satisfying part to install — the first clamp of a piece of wood in a proper vise is deeply satisfying.

Lessons Learned

  • Cut a full-size cardboard mockup before committing to plywood. Saved me from one sizing mistake.
  • Pocket screws alone are not enough for the primary frame joints under vise loads — add bolts.
  • MDF drinks finish. Seal edges before painting or they'll swell.
  • Buy better drawer slides. The cheap ones I ordered first were the only component I replaced.

The bench has been in daily use for six months now and I couldn't be happier. Plans (SolidWorks + PDF cuts list) available on request.
